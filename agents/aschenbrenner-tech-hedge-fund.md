# TECHNOLOGY HEDGE FUND MANAGER — Complete Investment Research, Portfolio Construction & Active Management (v5)

## OVERVIEW

This skill encodes a **complete investment management methodology** for translating a deep, first-principles technology vision into a concentrated, evolving portfolio — and then actively managing it through bottleneck migration, thesis validation, position rotation, social media surveillance, and operational monitoring.

It was reverse-engineered from studying how Leopold Aschenbrenner's "Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead" (June 2024) — a 165-page essay arguing AGI by 2027 is "strikingly plausible" — was translated into Situational Awareness LP, a fund that grew from $225M seed capital to $5.5B in four quarters (Q1-Q4 2025). The key insight: **the investment process begins with technology understanding, not financial analysis.** The technology vision IS the edge. Everything downstream — bottleneck identification, company selection, position construction, portfolio evolution — flows from the quality and depth of the vision.

## THE FOUNDATIONAL EPISTEMOLOGY — "Trust the Trendlines"

### The Core Axiom
Quantitative trendlines in technology are more reliable than expert narratives. When a capability metric has been compounding at a consistent rate for a decade, the default assumption is that it continues — unless you can identify a specific, structural reason it stops.

> "If you keep being surprised by AI capabilities, just start counting the OOMs."

This is the "counting the OOMs" principle (OOM = order of magnitude, 10x = 1 OOM):
- Identify the key capability metric for the technology
- Plot the historical trendline on a log scale
- Extrapolate forward 3-7 years
- Ask: "If this trendline continues, what does the world look like?"
- The people who correctly predicted recent advances "trusted the trendlines" while skeptics at every turn said "deep learning won't be able to do X" and were quickly proven wrong

### The Consensus Blindness Principle
> "Everyone is now talking about AI, but few have the faintest glimmer of what is about to hit them."

The market systematically underestimates exponential technology trends because:
- **Narrative anchoring**: "It's just predicting the next word" — focusing on current limitations rather than the trajectory
- **Linear extrapolation of exponential trends**: Pricing incremental improvement when trendlines imply transformative change
- **Recency bias**: A quiet year (models "in the oven") gets interpreted as "hitting a wall" rather than the next generation being built
- **Expert overconfidence**: "Predictions they've made every year for the last decade, and which they've been consistently wrong about"

### The Simplicity Trap
The best investment ideas from technology visions often appear deceptively simple — so simple that sophisticated investors dismiss them. "Obviously Bitcoin miners have permits and energy. Why would everyone not be buying these things?" The simplicity IS the edge. When an investment thesis can be explained in one sentence and sounds "too obvious," that is often a signal of a genuine edge — the market assumes someone else must already be exploiting it.

### The "Bits to Atoms" Transition
Technology-driven secular trends eventually hit a phase transition from software/digital to physical/manufacturing. When this happens:
- Software companies that only rely on software face disruption (AI can generate software)
- Physical infrastructure companies that own permits, land, power, and manufacturing capacity become the scarce layer
- The investment thesis shifts from "who writes the best code" to "who owns the atoms"
- Things that need human horsepower, permits, and legislation cannot be replicated by AI

### The Civilizational Stakes Framing
> "Perhaps they will go down in history like Szilard and Oppenheimer and Teller."

The essay frames AI development not as a business opportunity but as a civilizational inflection point comparable to the Manhattan Project. This framing explains the fund's extreme conviction and willingness to concentrate. When you believe the technology will "give a decisive economic and military advantage" and that "the free world's very survival will be at stake," portfolio diversification becomes a secondary concern. The stakes justify the concentration. It also explains the willingness to hold contrarian positions (Intel calls) for extended periods — if the thesis is about national survival, short-term price action is noise.

## PHASE 1: BUILD THE TECHNOLOGY VISION — "Count the OOMs"

### 1.1 The Three Drivers of Technology Progress
For any technology trend, decompose progress into three independent drivers:

```
DRIVER 1: SCALING — Raw resource scaling (compute, capital, capacity)
  - Measured in: dollars invested, units deployed, physical capacity added
  - AI example: Training compute grew ~0.5 OOMs/year, ~5x faster than Moore's Law
  - GPT-2 to GPT-4: ~3,000-10,000x more raw compute
  - "Many people assume this is simply due to Moore's Law. But we are seeing much more
    rapid scaleups — close to 5x the speed of Moore's Law — instead because of mammoth investment."
  - Question: Is the capital available to sustain this scaling?

DRIVER 2: EFFICIENCY — Algorithmic/design improvements that multiply the scaling
  - Measured in: performance per dollar, output per unit input
  - AI example: ~0.5 OOMs/year algorithmic efficiency (ImageNet data: consistent over 9 years)
  - Inference cost to achieve 50% on MATH dropped ~1,000x in <2 years
  - "Individual discoveries seem random, but the long-run trendline is predictable"
  - Question: Is there still headroom for efficiency improvements?

DRIVER 3: UNLOCKING — Paradigm shifts that unlock latent capability ("unhobbling")
  - Measured in: qualitative capability jumps, new use cases enabled
  - AI examples: RLHF (100x effective compute gain), Chain-of-Thought (>10x on math/reasoning),
    scaffolding (GPT-3.5 outperforms un-scaffolded GPT-4), tools, context length (2k→1M+ tokens),
    posttraining improvements (~100 ELO gain on existing model)
  - "The raw intelligence is mostly there, even if models are still artificially constrained"
  - Question: What obvious limitations exist today that will be solved?
```

### 1.2 The Capability Trajectory and Capability Ladder
Combine the three drivers to project the total effective capability improvement:

```
TOTAL EFFECTIVE IMPROVEMENT = Scaling × Efficiency × Unlocking

AI Example (GPT-2 to GPT-4, 4 years):
  Scaling: ~3.5-4 OOMs | Efficiency: ~1-2 OOMs | Unlocking: Large (RLHF, CoT, tools)
  Total: ~100,000x effective compute scaleup

AI Projection (GPT-4 to 2027, 4 more years):
  Scaling: +2-3 OOMs ($10B-$100B+ clusters) | Efficiency: +1-3 OOMs | Unlocking: Agents, self-play
  Total: Another ~100,000x → "Another GPT-2-to-GPT-4-sized qualitative jump"
```

**The Capability Ladder:**
```
CHATBOT (current) → AGENT (near-term) → "DROP-IN REMOTE WORKER" (AGI)

"That doesn't just mean a better chatbot; picking the many obvious low-hanging
fruit on 'unhobbling' gains should take us from chatbots to agents, from a tool
to something that looks more like drop-in remote worker replacements."
```

Each rung determines which industries get disrupted and when. The "disruption short" thesis (Phase 5.2) depends on correctly identifying which rung the technology has reached.

### 1.3 Address the Known Risks to the Trendline

```
RISK ASSESSMENT TEMPLATE:
Risk: [e.g., "Data wall — running out of internet data for training"]
Severity if realized: [e.g., "Trendline stalls, plateau at current capability"]
Probability of being solved: [e.g., "High — synthetic data, self-play, RL approaches"]
Evidence for optimism: [e.g., "AlphaGo precedent — self-play enabled superhuman performance"]
Evidence for pessimism: [e.g., "No published results yet, proprietary research"]
Net assessment: [e.g., "Injects variance but base case is labs crack it"]
```

**The "Missing Middle" Concept:** Look for the gap between what the technology CAN do in one mode and what it does in another. For AI: in-context learning is incredibly sample-efficient (Gemini 1.5 Pro learned Kalamang, a language spoken by <200 people, from a textbook placed in context — at human level), but pretraining is incredibly sample-INefficient (models skim the internet at light speed, spending most compute on "crap, like e-commerce or SEO"). The "missing middle" is: we can't yet "distill in-context learning back to the weights." If bridged, the upside is LARGER than the base case.

**The Textbook Analogy (communication tool):** Current LLMs skim a textbook at light speed. But when you or I read a dense math textbook, we read slowly, discuss, practice, fail, try again until it "clicks." Synthetic data and self-play are trying to give models this human-like learning process.

**The AlphaGo Two-Step Pattern (paradigm transition template):**
```
Step 1: IMITATION LEARNING — Train on human-generated data (current paradigm)
Step 2: SELF-PLAY/SELF-IMPROVEMENT — Model generates its own training data
  - Enables superhuman performance (AlphaGo's "move 37")
  - "Developing the equivalent of step 2 for LLMs is a key research problem"
  - When cracked: "will not just keep the scaling curves going, but possibly enable huge gains"
```

**The "Internet Is Mostly Crap" Bull Case Amplifier:** Current frontier models are trained on the internet — and the internet is mostly crap. If the data wall is cracked, models trained on entirely high-quality data could be dramatically better than trendline extrapolation suggests. The base case may be CONSERVATIVE.

### 1.4 The Lab Variance Prediction
As key algorithmic ideas become proprietary, expect increasing divergence between competitors:
- Labs' approaches will diverge much more than in the past
- "Even a lab that seems on the frontier now could get stuck while others race ahead"
- Open source will have a much harder time competing
- "When a lab figures it out, their breakthrough will be the key to AGI — one of the United States' most prized secrets"
- **Investment implication:** Creates a secondary thesis about WHICH lab wins and WHERE its infrastructure gets built. Reinforces the security/national security thesis.

### 1.5 The Vision Document
Write a structured technology vision document (minimum 10 pages, quantitative) answering:
1. Where are we today? Current state, measured quantitatively
2. What do the trendlines say? Extrapolate each driver forward with specific numbers
3. What does the world look like if trendlines hold? Concrete, falsifiable predictions
4. What could break the trendlines? Structural barriers with probability assessments
5. What is the consensus missing? The gap between trendlines and market pricing
6. What is the bull case amplifier? Why the base case might be conservative
7. Which competitors will diverge? Lab variance prediction and infrastructure implications

## PHASE 2: DERIVE PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCES — "What Does the Vision REQUIRE?"

### 2.1 The Second-Order Translation
Don't ask "who benefits from [technology]?" Ask: **"If my vision is correct, what must physically happen in the real world to make it possible?"**

```
PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCE CHAIN:
Technology Vision → Capability Requirement → Infrastructure Requirement → Physical Constraint

Example:
"AGI by 2027" → "100,000x compute scaleup" → "Trillion-dollar datacenter clusters" →
  → "Tens of percent growth in US electricity production"
  → "Hundreds of millions of GPUs need physical space, power, cooling"
  → "Every power contract, every voltage transformer must be secured"
  → "From the shale fields of Pennsylvania to the solar farms of Nevada"
```

### 2.2 The Capital Deployment Estimate
Quantify the capital that must flow:
- How many units of infrastructure are needed?
- What does each unit cost? (bottom-up engineering estimates, not top-down TAM)
- Over what timeline must they be deployed?
- Total capital deployment? (typically trillions for transformative trends)
- Validation: $650B committed to CapEx from just a few hyperscalers in a single earnings season

### 2.3 The Industrial Mobilization Frame
When capital deployment reaches sufficient scale, frame it as an **industrial mobilization**:
- Which industries will be transformed by the demand surge?
- Which supply chains will be stressed beyond capacity?
- Which government actions will be triggered?
- Which workforce and permitting constraints will become binding?

## PHASE 3: CONSTRUCT THE BOTTLENECK HIERARCHY — "What Takes Longest to Build?"

### 3.1 The Core Principle
**"What takes the longest to build, permit, or procure?"** That is the binding constraint. That is where the investment edge lives.

### 3.2 The Bottleneck Hierarchy Template

```
1. [Hardest — 3-10 years] Permits, physical construction, geological/geographic limits
   INVESTMENT: Highest conviction longs. Irreplaceable assets.

2. [Hard — 1-3 years] Specialized manufacturing, long lead times
   INVESTMENT: Strong longs. Supply constrained for medium term.

3. [Medium — 6-18 months] Capital and execution dependent
   INVESTMENT: Longs with lifecycle management. Monitor for capacity relief.

4. [Soft — weeks to months] Primarily capital allocation decisions
   INVESTMENT: Neutral. Not scarce enough for excess returns.

5. [No constraint] Abundant, commoditizing, or already solved
   INVESTMENT: Potential SHORT. Market overpricing scarcity that doesn't exist.
```

### 3.3 The Consensus Bottleneck vs. Actual Bottleneck
The market focuses on the **most visible** layer. The actual binding constraint is usually **less visible**.
- **LONG** the actual bottleneck (underpriced by the market)
- **SHORT** the consensus bottleneck (overpriced by the market)

### 3.4 The Portfolio as Constraint Map
The 13F filings serve as **a real-time tracker of where the fund sees the constraints.** Each quarterly filing reveals which bottleneck is currently binding. When positions rotate, it signals the constraint has migrated. Treat the portfolio not as a stock list but as a map of physical constraints ranked by severity.

### 3.5 The "Market Priced In" Transition
A bottleneck transitions from investable to "market priced in" when:
- The thesis becomes consensus
- Stock prices have re-rated to reflect the scarcity
- New capacity is being built to address the constraint
- The fund's positions in that layer are being exited

## PHASE 4: MAP THE VALUE CHAIN — "Where Is Scarcity?"

### 4.1 The Scarcity-Abundance Framework

| Layer | Scarcity | Investment Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Scarce + Hard | ★★★★★ | LONG — Core position, high conviction |
| Scarce + Medium | ★★★★ | LONG — Secondary position |
| Balanced | ★★★ | NEUTRAL — Monitor for shifts |
| Abundant + Growing | ★★ | POTENTIAL SHORT — Consensus overpriced |
| Abundant + Mature | ★ | SHORT — Consensus fully priced |

### 4.2 Conversion Play Identification — "The Liquor License Hack"

The most powerful conversion plays are like **buying a bar that already has a liquor license rather than applying for one.** You sidestep the entire permitting/construction bottleneck by acquiring companies that already have the scarce asset but are valued in a different paradigm.

```
CONVERSION PLAY CHECKLIST:
□ Long-term contracts for the scarce resource? (power PPAs, spectrum, land)
□ Permitted physical sites? (zoning, grid interconnection, environmental)
□ Relevant infrastructure? (cooling, networking, security)
□ Conversion economically rational? (higher $/MW or $/sqft from new use case)
□ Management signaled intent to convert? (earnings calls, partnerships)
□ Precedent deal signed? (validates conversion thesis for entire category)
□ Conversion timeline? (months, not years = better)
□ Can you "gut" the old business and repurpose for the new one?
□ Is the acquirer approaching activist-level ownership?
```

**Observed example:** BTC miners possess the three things AI datacenters need most: (1) cheap, long-term power purchase agreements, (2) physical sites with grid interconnection already permitted, (3) industrial cooling infrastructure. Converting to AI compute hosting is faster and cheaper than building greenfield. The Bitfarms-CoreWeave deal ($10.2B over 12 years) validated the thesis for the entire category.

### 4.3 Manufacturing Scalability Risk
When the thesis moves "into the world of atoms," assess: **can the company actually manufacture at scale?**

```
MANUFACTURING RISK TEMPLATE:
Company: [e.g., Bloom Energy]
Product: [e.g., Solid oxide fuel cells]
Current capacity: [e.g., ~X GW/year]
Demand backlog: [e.g., $20B]
Revenue growth: [e.g., 34% in 2025, projecting 40% in 2026]
Key question: Can they create enough product to put a dent in the backlog?
Manufacturing constraints: [raw materials, factory space, skilled labor, supply chain]
Scaling timeline: [months to years]
Risk level: [Low/Medium/High]
```

| "No startup can handle superintelligence" | Government will favor domestic champions | Calls (Intel — CHIPS Act) |

### 5.2 The Disruption Short
The vision document identifies what gets destroyed. Use the Capability Ladder (Phase 1.2) to determine which industries are disrupted at each rung:
- **Chatbot level:** Customer service, basic content generation
- **Agent level:** IT outsourcing, routine coding, data entry
- **Drop-in remote worker level:** Professional services, research, complex engineering

Short companies whose business model is providing cheaper human labor for tasks the technology can now automate. The Infosys put is about the PRODUCT of infrastructure (AI capability) destroying an existing business model (IT outsourcing).

### 5.3 Company Screening Criteria (7-Criterion Framework)

| Criterion | Question | Scoring |
|---|---|---|
| **Bottleneck Ownership** | Does this company own/control a scarce/constrained resource? | 1-5 (5 = monopoly) |
| **Scarcity Duration** | How long will this resource remain scarce? | 1-5 (5 = 5+ years) |
| **Timeline Robustness** | Does this work in fast/base/slow scenarios? | 1-5 (5 = all scenarios) |
| **Consensus Positioning** | Is the market underpricing or overpricing this? | -2 to +2 (positive = underpriced) |
| **Conversion Optionality** | Can existing assets pivot to serve the secular trend? | 0-3 (3 = massive potential) |
| **Government Tailwind** | Does policy (subsidies, mandates, security) help? | 0-3 (3 = direct beneficiary) |
| **Geopolitical Resilience** | Is this company in a geopolitically safe jurisdiction? | 0-2 (2 = domestic/allied) |

**Composite Score = Σ(all criteria). Max = 25.**
- Score ≥ 18: Core position candidate
- Score 12-17: Secondary position candidate
- Score 8-11: Monitor / exploratory
- Score < 8: Pass

```
1. DOMESTIC CHAMPIONS — Strategically irreplaceable for the home country
   Expression: Call options (contrarian + asymmetric + binary government catalyst)
   Example: Intel — the ONLY US-based advanced foundry

2. ALLIED SUPPLY CHAIN — Companies in allied jurisdictions providing critical inputs
   Expression: Common stock (steady conviction, geopolitically resilient)

3. ADVERSARY-EXPOSED — Companies with critical operations in adversary jurisdictions
   Expression: Put options (geopolitical risk hedge)
   Example: TSMC puts (Taiwan geopolitical risk)

4. DISRUPTION TARGETS — Industries/companies the technology will displace
   Expression: Small put positions (thesis-consistent, long-dated)
   Example: Infosys puts (AI disrupts IT outsourcing)
```

## PHASE 7: CONSTRUCT POSITIONS — "How Do I Express the Vision?"

### 7.1 Position Types and When to Use Each

| Expression | When to Use | Risk Profile | Fund Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Common stock (long)** | High conviction, thesis playing out | Standard | Bloom Energy, CoreWeave, Core Scientific |
| **Call options** | Contrarian thesis, consensus bearish, cheap IV, max asymmetry | Leveraged upside, defined downside | Intel, CoreWeave, Bloom Energy, EQT |
| **Put options (hedge)** | Consensus overpriced, tactical timing | Defined cost | NVIDIA, TSMC, Broadcom, SMH |
| **Put options (on own longs)** | Very large position needs downside protection | Insurance | CoreWeave puts in Q3 (hedging massive long) |
| **Sector ETF puts** | Broad sector hedge before identifying specific shorts | Diversified hedge | SMH puts in Q2 (replaced by individual puts in Q3) |

### 7.2 The Asymmetry Principle
Use options specifically when:
- **The thesis is contrarian:** Market disagrees → options cheap → re-rating dramatic if right
- **The thesis has a catalyst with timeline:** Government action, contract announcement
- **The position is a national security bet:** Binary outcomes
- **The company is deeply out of favor:** Consensus bearish = cheap implied vol = maximum asymmetry

### 7.3 The Anchor Position
Every portfolio should have one or more **anchor positions** — positions that embody the absolute core of the vision and DO NOT ROTATE:
- Held at a fixed size (share count unchanged quarter to quarter)
- Represents the single most irreplaceable asset in the vision
- Often expressed via call options for maximum asymmetry
- Survives all three timeline scenarios

**Observed example:** Intel call options held at EXACTLY 20,237,400 shares across all four quarters. The share count never changed. This is the domestic foundry = national security thesis — the single most persistent bet.

### 7.4 Position Sizing Rules (Observed from 13F Data)

```
SIZING FRAMEWORK:
- Anchor position(s): 10-46% of portfolio (varies inversely with total AUM growth)
- Core theme positions (top 3-5): 5-16% each
- Secondary positions: 1-5% each
- Exploratory/new positions: 0.2-1% each
- Hedges/shorts: 1-27% each (varies by conviction and timing)
- Single position max: ~20% of portfolio
- Single theme max: ~42% of portfolio
- Total hedge/short exposure: 5-30% (peaked Q2-Q3, minimal by Q4)
```

### 7.5 Capital Efficiency Techniques

**The Equity-to-Calls Liquidity Hack:** Sell equity while keeping call options open. Frees liquidity without reducing directional exposure.

**Observed example:** Intel — sold equity (1 share remaining) but maintained 20,237,400-share call position. Freed hundreds of millions for new positions.

### 7.6 Activist Investor Dynamics

When position sizes approach ~10% ownership, the fund can **influence company outcomes.** This creates a feedback loop: buy → influence → accelerate conversion → value creation → position appreciates. Monitor ownership via 13F filings and 13D/13G thresholds.

## PHASE 9: MONITOR & ADAPT — "Is the Vision Still Correct?"

### 9.1 Thesis Validation Signals
Track: trendline integrity (is the core capability metric still on trend?), capital deployment (are companies still investing?), bottleneck evidence (wait times, pricing, queue lengths), government action (subsidies, mandates, export controls), conversion deals (BTC miner → AI datacenter), consensus shift (is the market catching up?), manufacturing capacity (can key companies scale production?).

### 9.2 Kill Signals
- Core trendline breaks structurally (not a temporary pause)
- Physical limit reached, cannot be engineered around
- Bottleneck solved faster than expected (new technology, regulatory change)
- Government policy reverses (subsidies cut, mandates removed)
- Substitute technology bypasses entire value chain
- Market fully prices the vision (no remaining edge)
- Manufacturing execution failure at key companies
- The essay's specific predictions are falsified (e.g., no AGI-level models by 2028, no government project, no trillion-dollar clusters)

### 9.3 Single-Thesis Correlation Risk
If the secular trend pauses, every position correlates to the downside. Mitigate via:
- Timeline robustness gate (positions work even if delayed)
- Tactical hedges (puts on consensus names)
- Exit discipline (don't hold re-rated positions)
- Physical infrastructure salvage value (datacenters have value regardless)
- Committed CapEx visibility ($650B+ provides multi-year demand floor)

### 9.4 Quarterly Review Checklist
1. Trendline integrity → still on track?
2. Bottleneck migration → has the constraint shifted?
3. Position sizing → drift from targets?
4. Hedge book → adequate? expired? need refresh?
5. New opportunities → companies entering the bottleneck layer?
6. Exit candidates → positions that have re-rated?
7. Vision scorecard → essay predictions vs reality
8. Manufacturing check → can key companies scale?
9. Ownership thresholds → any positions approaching 5-10% ownership?

| +0.3 to +0.7 | Moderately bullish | FAVORABLE: Thesis gaining traction but not yet crowded. |
| -0.3 to +0.3 | Neutral / mixed | MONITOR: No strong signal. |
| -0.7 to -0.3 | Moderately bearish | CONTRARIAN OPPORTUNITY: If fundamentals intact, this is where the edge lives. |
| < -0.7 | Extreme bearish | HIGH ALERT: Either thesis is broken or maximum contrarian opportunity. |

### 10.3 Sentiment Divergence Signals (Most Actionable)
- Bullish sentiment + falling price = smart money exiting
- Bearish sentiment + rising price = institutional buying
- Tier 1-2 bearish + Tier 4-5 bullish = insiders see something
- Volume spike + neutral sentiment = information asymmetry
- Sentiment reversal (flips sign within 1 week) = narrative shift in progress

### 10.4 Novel Information Detection
What constitutes alpha: (1) validates/invalidates essay predictions, (2) reveals infrastructure constraints before earnings, (3) signals deal flow before announcements, (4) shifts the bottleneck, (5) changes geopolitical calculus.

### 10.5 Anti-Patterns — What to Ignore
Retail pump/dump chatter, meme stock energy, AI doomer threads, influencer stock picks, crypto maximalist BTC takes, political partisan framing, "AI is just hype" takes, competitor bashing.

**Rule:** Never change a position based solely on social media. It triggers investigation, not position changes.

## THE THREE-LAYER INVESTMENT PROCESS

### Layer 1: Hard Quantitative Gates (Binary Pass/Fail)
Every position must pass ALL five gates:

| Gate | Test | Rationale |
|------|------|-----------|
| **Bottleneck Proximity** | Does the company directly address a physical constraint? | Thesis is about physical bottlenecks, not software |
| **Scarcity** | Is the asset/capability scarce and difficult to replicate? | Abundant assets don't command pricing power |
| **Timeline Robustness** | Does the thesis work across fast/medium/delayed timelines? | Datacenters need power regardless of when AGI arrives |
| **Asymmetry** | Is the risk/reward skewed? Can you lose 1x but make 5-10x? | Options and out-of-favor names provide asymmetry |
| **Domestic/Allied** | Is the company US-based or in an allied nation? | "The free world must prevail" |

### Layer 2: Qualitative Rules
1. **"Literal reading"** — Take the thesis claims literally and find the matching company
2. **Conversion play ID** — Find assets valued in one paradigm, re-valued in another ("Liquor License Hack")
3. **Consensus gap** — Own what the market undervalues; short what it overvalues
4. **Position expression** — Match instrument to thesis type (calls for contrarian, stock for conviction, puts for hedges)
5. **Rotation readiness** — Exit positions that re-rated; rotate into next bottleneck
6. **Disruption short** — Short companies whose business model the technology destroys

### Layer 3: Contextual Heuristics
1. "Bottleneck migrates downstream" — First chips, then power, then physical sites
2. "Own the longest lead time" — Power plants (2-5yr) > fabs (3-5yr) > datacenters (1-2yr) > GPUs (months)
3. "Government picks winners" — CHIPS Act → Intel; nuclear restarts → utilities; defense AI → domestic
4. "When everyone owns it, short it" — NVIDIA was the most crowded trade in 2024-2025
5. "Find the hidden infrastructure" — BTC miners have power + permits but valued as crypto
6. "The simpler it sounds, the better it might be" — Simplicity IS the edge
7. "Bits to atoms" — When the thesis moves to physical manufacturing, own the atoms

## ANTI-PATTERNS — What NOT to Do

1. **Don't start with stocks.** Start with the technology. The vision IS the edge.
2. **Don't trust narratives over trendlines.** "It's just predicting the next word" is a narrative. The trendline says otherwise.
3. **Don't buy the consensus trade.** If everyone already owns it, the edge is gone.
4. **Don't confuse the narrative with the bottleneck.** "AI is the future" is a narrative. "Power generation takes 3-5 years" is a bottleneck.
5. **Don't hold positions after they re-rate.** Once the market agrees, rotate. (Vistra, Constellation, Talen — all exited.)
6. **Don't make permanent shorts.** Puts are tactical hedges with defined timelines. (NVIDIA puts held Q3 only.)
7. **Don't diversify away conviction.** 29 positions with 42% in one theme is fine. 100 positions with 1% each is not vision-driven.
8. **Don't ignore geopolitics.** Government action can create or destroy bottlenecks overnight.
9. **Don't anchor to entry price.** If thesis strengthens, add to winners. (Bloom Energy scaled 21x in one quarter.)
10. **Don't treat the vision as static.** Technology evolves. Bottlenecks migrate. Quarterly reviews are mandatory.
11. **Don't confuse social media noise for signal.** Filter retail pump/dump, meme energy, doomer threads.
12. **Don't act on social media alone.** It triggers investigation, not position changes.
13. **Don't dismiss ideas because they sound too simple.** The Simplicity Trap — simplicity IS the edge.
14. **Don't forget manufacturing risk when the thesis moves to atoms.** Can the company actually scale production?
15. **Don't ignore activist potential when ownership crosses 5-10%.** Influence creates value.
16. **Don't assume the base case is the ceiling.** The "internet is mostly crap" insight means cracking the data wall could produce models far better than trendline extrapolation suggests.
