## The 5-Stage Big Debt Cycle

### Stage 1 — Sound Money
Net debt low; money is "hard"; Debt/GDP and debt service/revenue low; equities cheap vs. bonds; **Ferguson check:** defense spending comfortably exceeds debt service.

### Stage 2 — The Debt Bubble
Debt/investment growth exceeds incomes; assets overpriced; "this time is different" meme; widespread leverage. **Bubble identifier checklist:** debt growth >3yr >income growth, high asset prices vs. PV of cash flows, new buyers, broad bullishness, leverage, forward speculation, expanding to meet unsustainable demand.

### Stage 3 — The Top
Tightening of money + prior debt growth becomes unsustainable. Self-reinforcing contraction. **Key signal:** long-term yields rising faster than short + currency weakening simultaneously. **Ferguson check:** debt service approaching/exceeding defense.

### Stage 4 — The Bust / Depression
Private debt crisis → government bailout → government debt rises sharply. **Dalio's law: "In the end, the central bank always prints and devalues."** Four levers: austerity, debt restructuring, wealth redistribution, debt monetization. **Beautiful deleveraging** = balanced; nominal growth > nominal interest rates. **Ferguson Limit crossed:** debt service > defense spending.

### Stage 5 — Reset / New Equilibrium
Debt brought back in line with incomes via inflationary depression. Currency re-anchored. Very high real interest rates restore credibility. New cycle begins.

## The 9-Stage Final Crisis Sequence (Risk Checklist)

1. Private sector and government get deep in debt
2. Private sector debt crisis; government goes deeper
3. Government debt squeeze (the critical inflection — net selling of govt debt)
4. Selling of government debt creates simultaneous tightening
5. Central bank prints money and buys bonds (QE)
6. Central bank goes broke (negative carry death spiral)
7. Debt restructuring and devaluation (beautiful deleveraging attempt)
8. Extraordinary policies imposed (wealth taxes, capital controls, financial repression)
9. Return to equilibrium; new cycle can begin

## Monetary Policy Phases

| Phase | Name | Mechanism | Key Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MP0** | Hard / Linked Money | Currency pegged to gold/another hard asset | Abrupt devaluation when promise broken |
| **MP1** | Fiat / Interest-Rate Driven | Central bank uses rates as primary tool | Rates hit zero bound |
| **MP2** | Fiat + Debt Monetization (QE) | CB prints and buys bonds | Negative carry, currency debasement |
| **MP3** | Coordinated Fiscal + Monetization | Large deficits explicitly financed by money creation | Inflationary spiral, loss of faith in currency |
| **Deleveraging** | Restructuring + Monetization | Four levers deployed | Beautiful vs. ugly outcome |
| **Reset** | Return to Hard Money | High real rates; currency re-anchored | New cycle begins |

**Historical anchors:** MP0→MP1 (1971 Nixon), MP1→MP2 (2008 Fed QE1), MP2→MP3 (2020 COVID).

## Ferguson's Law (The Geopolitical Tipping Point)

**The Ferguson Limit:** The threshold at which interest payments on the public debt exceed defense outlays. After this:
- The debt burden draws scarce resources toward itself
- Amount available for national security is reduced
- The power becomes vulnerable to military challenge
- Strategic rivals are incentivized to challenge the power's position

**US 2024 fact:** Net interest payments (~3.1% GDP) exceeded defense spending (~3.0–3.4% GDP) — first time in nearly a century. Trajectory: defense declining (3.2%→2.5%); interest rising (2.9%→3.3%).

**Four ways out (Ferguson):** Primary surpluses (UK 1815-1914) | Falling real rates (UK 1930s) | Financial repression (UK 1950s-60s) | Strategic retreat (UK post-WWII).

**Apply the Ferguson check on every great-power analysis.**

## Reserve Currency Mechanics

Reserve currency status delays but does NOT eliminate debt-sustainability constraints. Lost via: chronic fiscal deficits, sanctions weaponization, political instability, debt monetization, credible alternatives, loss of geopolitical dominance.

**Credit rating agency blind spot:** Rate default risk only — not devaluation risk. For countries with debt in their own currency, the real risk is debasement, not default.

## Late-Cycle Warning Dashboard (30+ checkboxes — use this)

### Debt stock
- Govt debt/GDP elevated and rising (>80% developed; >60% emerging)
- Govt debt/revenue elevated (>150% historically associated with debt busts)
- Debt denominated in foreign currency
- Central bank holds large % of outstanding debt (>20%)
- Liquid government assets low relative to obligations

### Flow / fiscal
- Structural fiscal deficit large and persistent (>3% GDP)
- Government interest expense as % of revenue rising (>20% elevated; >40% critical)
- Deficit projected to widen further
- Debt service growing faster than revenue

### Ferguson's Law
- Debt service as % GDP approaching/exceeding defense spending as % GDP
- Ferguson Limit crossed
- Defense spending declining as % GDP while debt service rising
- Military capability degrading vs. rivals
- Entitlement spending crowding out defense AND debt service
- Demographic deterioration making entitlement reform politically impossible

### Market / price
- Long-term yields rising faster than short-term rates
- Currency weakening simultaneously with rising yields (capital flight)
- Real yields deeply negative
- Credit spreads on government debt widening
- Gold and hard assets outperforming local-currency bonds

### Monetary policy
- Central bank in MP2 or MP3 phase
- Central bank balance sheet large vs. GDP (>30%)
- Central bank running negative carry
- Money supply growing faster than real GDP + inflation target

### Structural
- Reserve currency status being undermined
- Foreign holdings of govt debt declining
- Capital controls being considered or imposed
- Extraordinary taxes or wealth levies being discussed

### Political / geopolitical
- Domestic political polarization high; fiscal compromise impossible
- Geopolitical conflict escalating
- Nature shocks adding to fiscal burden
- Tech disruption redistributing income/wealth in destabilizing ways
- Strategic rivals compliant with Ferguson's Law while subject is not

**Scoring:** 0-5 manageable | 6-12 elevated | 13-20 crisis zone | 20+ acute crisis (Stage 5-6).

## Eisman Private Credit Framework (Stage 2 → 3 transition signal)

**Software concentration:** WSJ found self-reported software exposure understated by 6-9 pp. Actual ~25%+ of all direct lending loans go to software companies bought 2018-2022 by PE.

**Three opacity layers:** sector classification (self-classify, no taxonomy), mark-to-market (priced by funds themselves, no liquid secondary), structural (Investment Company Act exemptions, no leverage limits, no liquidity).

**GFC arrogance parallel:** GFC came from leverage; private credit arrogance comes from absence of credit cycle (17 years of below-normal losses = false confidence).

**Contagion transmission:** Banks (financed PE equity AND credit) → Pension funds (yield-seeking) → Life insurers → Trust erosion (entire asset class).

**Stage 3 → 4 signals to watch:** Rising defaults in PE-backed software, private credit reducing marks, banks announcing writedowns on leveraged loans, pension fund NAV declines, redemptions approaching 5% quarterly gate.

**Reconciliation with NBER WP 34991:** NBER finds high E/A ratios (0.65–0.85 vs. banks 0.11) reduces individual-fund fragility. But 5 vulnerability channels remain: cyclicality, bank linkages, LP transmission, valuation contagion, regulatory perimeter. Both can be true: low individual leverage, high systemic fragility.

## Fed Inflation Diagnosis Framework (Kakhbod/Kermani/Maciel)

**Master rule:**
```
DEMAND DIAGNOSIS → TRADE THE FED PATH (front-end, bear-flatten)
SUPPLY DIAGNOSIS → TRADE RISK PREMIA (long-end, bear-steepen, ERP widens)
```

**Fed reaction-function asymmetry (Taylor-rule estimates):**
- Inflation overall: 1.37
- Demand imbalance increment: **1.66** (large, significant)
- Supply imbalance increment: **0.32** (small, weak)

The Fed leans against demand inflation aggressively and looks through supply inflation.

**Yield curve loadings:**
- Demand → risk-neutral yields (front-end most affected)
- Supply → term premia (long-end, ~0.052 at 10y)

**Three questions at every FOMC event:**
1. What kind of inflation is this?
2. How will the Fed react?
3. How will the market express it?

**Stagflation danger zone (late Big Cycle):** When in Stage 3-4, currency weakness → import pass-through → supply inflation → Fed faces trade-off → term premium rises → stock-bond diversification breaks down.
