## PART II: THE CORE INVESTMENT PHILOSOPHY

### 2.1 The Foundational Axioms (Non-Negotiable)

**Axiom 1: Read the primary source.**
Never rely on sell-side summaries, news articles, or consensus narratives. Go to the 10-K, the prospectus, the covenant package, the footnotes. The truth is always in the document that nobody reads.

**Axiom 2: The market is often wrong, sometimes catastrophically so.**
Burry is not an efficient market believer. He believes prices can diverge from intrinsic value for years — and that this divergence is the source of alpha. The divergence is largest when the structural flaw is complex, hidden in legal documents, or requires cross-disciplinary knowledge to understand.

**Axiom 3: Supply-side excess is the cardinal sign of a bubble.**
Demand-side enthusiasm is visible and priced in. Supply-side overbuilding is structural and slow to correct. When producers massively overbuild capacity in anticipation of demand that never materializes at scale, the collapse is inevitable and severe. This is the unifying framework across: dot-com (fiber, servers), housing (mortgage origination, CDO issuance), and AI (GPU clusters, data centers).

**Axiom 4: Accounting is a language — learn to read it critically.**
Reported earnings are a management choice, not a fact. Depreciation schedules, revenue recognition, off-balance-sheet vehicles, and covenant definitions are all discretionary. The gap between reported earnings and cash reality is where the trade lives.

**Axiom 5: Be early, stay patient, size correctly.**
Being right too early is indistinguishable from being wrong — until it isn't. The position must be sized so you can survive the period of being early. Burry held his GFC short for 2+ years while paying CDS premiums and facing investor redemptions. He did not capitulate.

**Axiom 6: Structural flaws compound.**
A structural problem does not correct linearly. It builds, gets papered over, builds more, and then corrects violently. The longer the structural flaw persists, the larger the eventual correction.

**Axiom 7: Consensus is not evidence.**
The fact that everyone believes something is not evidence it is true. In fact, universal belief in a thesis is often evidence that the trade is crowded and the risk/reward has deteriorated.

### 2.2 What Burry Is NOT
- Not a macro trader (he does not trade currencies, rates, or commodities as primary instruments)
- Not a momentum investor
- Not a technical analyst
- Not a quant (no black-box models — everything is fundamental and structural)
- Not a short-term trader (his holding periods are measured in years)
- Not a diversifier (he runs concentrated books — his GFC trade was the majority of his fund)

## PART III: THE GFC TRADE — COMPLETE DECISION PROCESS

### 3.1 How He Found It (2003–2005)

**Step 1: Read what nobody reads.**
Burry began reading mortgage prospectuses and pooling & servicing agreements (PSAs) in 2003–2004 — documents that ran hundreds of pages and that virtually no investor on Wall Street had read in full. He was looking for the actual legal structure of mortgage-backed securities, not the rating agency summary.

**Step 2: Identify the structural flaw.**
What he found: mortgage underwriting standards had collapsed. Loans were being made to borrowers with no income verification, no asset verification, adjustable rates that would reset sharply, and loan-to-value ratios that assumed perpetual home price appreciation. The loans were being pooled into MBS and CDOs, rated AAA by agencies that were paid by the issuers, and sold to investors who trusted the ratings without reading the underlying documents.

**Step 3: Quantify the gap.**
Burry built models showing that if home prices merely stopped rising (not even fell), the default rates on subprime mortgages would be catastrophic — far exceeding what the AAA ratings implied. The market was pricing near-zero probability of default on instruments that had near-certain default embedded in them.

**Step 4: Find the instrument.**
The insight was not just that housing would fall — it was finding the *right instrument* to express the trade. Burry approached Goldman Sachs, Deutsche Bank, and others to create **credit default swaps (CDS) on mortgage-backed securities** — essentially insurance contracts that paid out if the underlying mortgages defaulted. These instruments barely existed. He had to convince banks to create them.

**Step 5: Size the position.**
Burry allocated a significant portion of Scion's capital to CDS premiums — ongoing costs that had to be paid while waiting for the thesis to play out. He understood this was a **carry trade with negative cash flow** until the event occurred. He sized it so the fund could survive 2+ years of premium payments.

### 3.2 The Holding Period (2005–2007) — How He Survived Being Early

**The problem:** After initiating the trade in 2005, housing continued to rise. CDS premiums were a constant cash drain. Investors began demanding redemptions. His own investors questioned his sanity. The banks he bought protection from tried to mark his positions against him.

**How he held:**
1. **The thesis had not changed.** He re-read the prospectuses. The loans were still bad. The ratings were still wrong. Nothing in the primary documents had changed — only the market price.
2. **He locked up investor capital.** He imposed redemption restrictions on his fund — a deeply unpopular move that cost him relationships but preserved the position.
3. **He separated signal from noise.** Market price moving against him was noise. Loan performance data was signal. He tracked actual mortgage delinquency data, not market prices.
4. **He wrote to his investors.** Detailed, analytical letters explaining exactly why he was right and the market was wrong. He did not hide from the disagreement — he documented it.
5. **He did not add to the position recklessly.** He maintained discipline on sizing even when conviction was high.

### 3.3 The Resolution (2007–2008)

When subprime mortgage delinquencies began rising in 2006–2007, the CDS positions exploded in value. Scion returned ~490% net of fees on the trade. Burry personally made ~$100M.

**What he did after:**
- Returned capital to investors
- Closed the fund
- Went quiet for years
- Re-emerged periodically with concentrated positions (Japanese stocks, water rights, gold miners, Chinese ADRs)
- Ultimately closed Scion entirely in 2025 to write *Cassandra Unchained*

### 3.4 The GFC Trade — Analytical Checklist (Reconstructed)

When Burry evaluates a potential structural short, he implicitly runs through:

```
□ Is there a primary source document that nobody is reading?
□ Does the legal/accounting structure create a gap between 
  perception and reality?
□ Are rating agencies, analysts, or consensus relying on

□ What is the specific trigger that will force the market 
  to recognize the structural flaw?
□ Has the thesis changed, or only the price?
```

## PART V: BURRY'S ANALYTICAL PROCESS — STEP BY STEP

### 5.1 The Research Protocol

**Phase 1: Find the ignored document**
Every Burry trade starts with a document that the market has not read or has misread:
- GFC: mortgage prospectuses and pooling & servicing agreements
- AI bubble: hyperscaler 10-Ks and depreciation footnotes
- Tesla: the actual compensation plan documents and dilution math
- Chinese tech: VIE structure legal agreements
- GameStop: balance sheet and Cohen's ownership filings

**Phase 2: Identify the structural gap**
What does the document reveal that the market price does not reflect?
- Is there a legal structure that creates hidden risk?
- Is there an accounting choice that inflates reported earnings?
- Is there a supply/demand imbalance that is being rationalized?
- Is there a valuation assumption that requires perpetual perfection?

**Phase 3: Build the quantitative case**
Burry always quantifies:
- GFC: modeled default rates at various home price scenarios
- AI: calculated $176B depreciation understatement
- Tesla: computed 3.6% annual dilution, 209x forward P/E vs. 22x S&P
- Tencent: 5x revenue growth, 0% stock return — quantified the VIE discount

**Phase 4: Find the historical parallel**
Burry is a student of financial history. Every current thesis maps to a historical precedent:
- AI bubble → dot-com bubble (supply-side gluttony, Cisco/Nvidia parallel)
- Passive investing → any era where mechanical buying divorced price from value
- VIE shells → any structure where legal form diverges from economic substance
- Tesla dilution → any company where compensation exceeds value creation

**Phase 5: Identify the instrument**
What is the most efficient way to express the thesis?
- GFC: CDS on MBS (had to be invented/negotiated)
- Current: writing (no position constraints, no regulatory muzzle)
- Historical: concentrated equity longs (value stocks), equity puts, CDS

**Phase 6: Size for survival**
The position must survive the period of being early. Burry's rule: if the thesis is correct but the timing is uncertain, size so you can hold for 2+ years without being forced out.

**Phase 7: Monitor the thesis, not the price**
Price moving against you is not evidence you are wrong. The thesis is wrong only if the primary source documents change — if the structural flaw is corrected, if the accounting is restated, if the supply/demand imbalance resolves.

### 5.2 The Bubble Detection Framework

Burry's checklist for identifying a bubble:

```
SUPPLY-SIDE SIGNALS (most important):
□ Are producers massively overbuilding capacity?
□ Is the overbuilding being funded by cheap/easy credit?
□ Is there a "picks and shovels" supplier whose revenue 
  depends entirely on the buildout continuing?
□ Are producers rationalizing the buildout with demand 
  projections that require everything to go right?

ACCOUNTING SIGNALS:
□ Are companies extending asset life assumptions to 
  reduce depreciation?
□ Is revenue being recognized aggressively?
□ Are off-balance-sheet vehicles hiding leverage?
□ Is the gap between reported earnings and free cash flow 
  widening?

VALUATION SIGNALS:
□ Are multiples at or near historical extremes?
□ Does the current price require perpetual high growth 
  AND near-zero discount rates to be justified?
□ Is the "it's different this time" argument being made 
  by pointing to profitability of current leaders 
  (ignoring that prior bubble leaders were also profitable)?

STRUCTURAL SIGNALS:
□ Is there a mechanical, valuation-blind buyer 
  (passive funds, 401k auto-investment)?
□ Is price discovery broken?
□ Are rating agencies or analysts being paid by the 
  entities they are rating/covering?
□ Is the legal structure creating a gap between what 
  investors think they own and what they actually own?

SENTIMENT SIGNALS (least important, but confirming):
□ Is retail participation at extreme levels?
□ Is the consensus argument "you can't lose"?
□ Are critics being ridiculed rather than engaged?
```

## PART VI: POSITION MANAGEMENT & PSYCHOLOGY

### 6.1 How Burry Handles Being Early

The GFC trade was underwater for 2+ years. His framework for surviving:

1. **Separate price from thesis.** Price is what the market thinks today. Thesis is what the documents say. If the documents haven't changed, the thesis hasn't changed.

2. **Lock up capital if necessary.** Burry imposed redemption restrictions on Scion investors during the GFC trade. This was deeply unpopular but preserved the position. He was willing to lose investors to keep the trade.

3. **Write it down.** Detailed investor letters explaining the thesis in full. This served two purposes: it forced him to stress-test his own reasoning, and it created a record that he was right for the right reasons.

4. **Track the signal, not the noise.** During the GFC trade, he tracked actual mortgage delinquency data — not CDS spreads, not housing prices, not media coverage. The signal was in the loan performance data.

5. **Do not add recklessly.** High conviction does not mean unlimited sizing. The position must be survivable.

### 6.2 When Burry Exits

Burry exits when:
- The thesis has played out (the structural flaw has been recognized by the market)
- The primary source documents have changed (the structural flaw has been corrected)
- The risk/reward has deteriorated (the trade is now consensus, the easy money has been made)
- He is wrong (the documents reveal he misread the structure)

He does NOT exit because:
- The price moves against him (if the thesis is intact)
- Other investors disagree
- The media ridicules him
- The trade is uncomfortable

### 6.3 Burry on Conviction vs. Certainty

Burry operates with **high conviction but acknowledged uncertainty**. He does not claim to know when a bubble will pop — only that it will. His GFC trade was not a timing call; it was a structural call. He bought protection and waited.

This is a critical distinction: **Burry is not a market timer. He is a structural analyst who waits for the structure to assert itself.**

**What to look for in 10-K footnotes (Burry's specific targets):**
- **Depreciation schedules**: Note 1 or Note 2 (Summary of Significant Accounting Policies). Look for "useful life" of servers, GPUs, networking equipment. Compare year-over-year changes.
- **Revenue recognition**: When and how revenue is booked. Aggressive recognition = inflated near-term earnings.
- **Off-balance-sheet commitments**: Operating lease obligations, unconsolidated VIEs, contingent liabilities.
- **Stock-based compensation**: Total SBC as % of revenue and net income. Diluted share count trajectory.
- **Related party transactions**: Transactions between the company and insiders or affiliated entities.
- **Going concern language**: Any auditor qualification is a red flag.
