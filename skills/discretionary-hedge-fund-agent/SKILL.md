# Catalyst-Driven Hedge Fund: Investment & Trading Methodology

*Distilled from quarterly letter (4Q25) and investment framework playbook*

---

## 1. Fund Characterization

**Type:** Deep-value, catalyst-driven, long/short equity hedge fund with opportunistic credit and selective activism.

**Key distinction:** This is NOT a directional, macro, or statistical fund. Every position requires an identifiable catalyst — a specific event that can close the gap between price and intrinsic value.

**Mandate:** Concentrated long/short equity + opportunistic credit + occasional activism. Flexible across instruments (equity, credit, pairs, baskets, holdco/opco).

**Typical exposures (from 4Q25):**
- Long: 184% (equity), 193% (including credit)
- Short: 110%
- Gross: 294-303%
- Net: 74-83%
- Beta-adjusted net: 36%

**In one sentence:** Find a large gap between price and intrinsic value, verify the balance sheet can survive until the thesis is realized, map the specific event that closes the gap, and for shorts, identify the mechanism that forces the market to confront reality.

## 2. Five Core Principles

| # | Principle | Discipline |
|---|-----------|------------|
| 1 | **Cheap is not enough** | A security can be statistically cheap and still uninvestable if time works against the shareholder. Require balance-sheet survivability AND at least one tangible catalyst. |
| 2 | **Incentives come first** | Map compensation, ownership, external-management structures, and fee economics BEFORE modeling upside. Who gets paid for AUM vs. performance? |
| 3 | **Downside before upside** | Build a floor value first (liquidation value, cash, hard assets), then layer in upside. Emphasize per-share downside protection. |
| 4 | **Per-share math beats empire-building** | Model per-share accretion (FCF/share, buybacks, asset sales) rather than headline EBITDA growth. Reject empire-building M&A. |
| 5 | **Shorts need a reveal mechanism** | Never short on valuation alone. Require a specific mechanism that forces the market to confront hidden weakness (dividend cut, gate, failed refi, non-accrual spike, forced sale below marks). |

**Optimization target:** Asymmetry — high-confidence floor on downside, credible way to close the value gap, market not yet pricing the change. Mirror image for shorts: reported economics look better than real economics, and a future event can force marks/spreads/flows to move against the company.

## 3. Seven Analytical Pillars

| Pillar | Focus | Key Questions |
|--------|-------|---------------|
| 1. Incentive Analysis | Who benefits from growth, leverage, fees, or high marks? | Compensation decks, proxy filings, external management structures, FRE vs performance fee split |
| 2. Forensic Quant Analysis | Separate economic reality from reported numbers | Cash conversion quality, PIK detection, EBITDA add-back forensics, leverage layering, mark-to-market gap analysis |
| 3. Historical Pattern Matching | Find analogues for how similar situations resolved | SVB → private credit template; prior credit cycles; sector-specific precedents |
| 4. Asymmetric Trade Construction | Structure for convex payoff | 3:1+ risk/reward, multi-instrument expression, pair trades to isolate alpha |
| 5. Catalyst Identification & Timing | Map specific events that close the value gap | Buyback, break-up, deleveraging, asset sale, strategic review, regulatory change, pipeline readout |
| 6. Per-Share Value Discipline | Focus on value creation per share, not aggregate | FCF/share accretion from buybacks, asset sales, cost cuts; reject dilutive empire-building |
| 7. Governance & Activism | Governance as part of valuation | Board independence, ownership alignment, activism as last resort to unlock value |

## 4. Six-Stage Gated Research Workflow

Each stage has explicit kill criteria. The process prevents analysts from falling in love with stories before proving survivability, cash quality, and catalyst path.

| Stage | Required Output | Kill Criteria |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| 1. Idea Sourcing | Screened candidate list, event log, or sector map | Positioning-only ideas with no valuation gap or dislocation |
| 2. Fundamental Work | Business summary, industry map, management assessment | No durable moat, no credible unit economics, no path to survival |
| 3. Forensic Test | Cash-conversion model, debt ladder, accounting quality memo | Earnings unsupported by cash, covenant risk too high, borrow/financing impossible |
| 4. Underwrite Value | Base, bull, and floor valuation with assumptions deck | No compelling upside or floor too uncertain |
| 5. Catalyst & Sizing | Catalyst map, timing view, position recommendation | Catalyst too vague, too distant, or highly dependent on market beta |
| 6. Monitor & Exit | Dashboard of KPIs and reveal events | Thesis disproven → watch-only or exit |

**Hard rules:**
- Do NOT write a full investment memo until the forensic test is complete
- Do NOT size a position until the catalyst path is explicit
- Do NOT keep a weak thesis alive because it is optically cheap

## 5. Quantitative Screening & Valuation

### Core Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Use |
|--------|---------|-----|
| Base intrinsic value | Normalized FCF/share × exit multiple, or SOTP, or DCF | Conservative margins, normalize one-time costs |
| Floor value | Net cash + realizable assets − wind-down costs − debt | Critical for stressed or activist situations |
| Expected value | P(upside) × upside − P(downside) × downside | Use ranges, not point estimates |
| True cash coverage | Cash interest received / cash distribution paid | Exclude PIK and temporary financing optics |
| Stressed leverage | Net debt / downside EBITDA | Recompute with lower EBITDA and lower multiples |
| Per-share accretion | (Post-action FCF/share − pre-action FCF/share) / pre-action FCF/share | Apply to buybacks, asset sales, acquisitions |

### Screening Thresholds

- **Value gap:** >25% discount to base value for longs; premium to realistic value for shorts
- **Balance sheet:** Acceptable leverage under DOWNSIDE case, not management guidance
- **Cash conversion:** Low gap between reported EBITDA and FCF after working capital, capex, taxes, stock comp
- **Catalyst math:** Credible event within 6-18 months unless floor value is unusually strong
- **Per-share discipline:** Actions must improve value per share, not just aggregate EBITDA

### Short-Side Quant Disciplines

- Track reported income vs. cash income
- Track leverage on-balance-sheet AND through structured/temporary financing
- Track distribution coverage excluding PIK and unrealized marks
- Track asset-liability mismatch: how fast liabilities can move vs. how slowly assets can be sold

## 6. Long-Side Methodology: Five Archetypes

Each long idea follows one of these patterns:

### Archetype 1: FCF Inflection + Capital Allocation
**Pattern:** Company generating significant and growing FCF, deploying it accretively (buybacks, acquisitions at attractive prices), with per-share value compounding.
**Example:** $40-45 FCF/share, acquisitions at >15% FCF/share accretion, 15x multiple → $600+ target. "Flywheel strategy of accretively deploying free cash flow into assets."

### Archetype 2: Operational Turnaround + Pipeline
**Pattern:** Company with stable base business, temporary headwind creating discount, pipeline catalysts that can re-rate the stock.
**Example:** Stable base growing 1-2% CC, FDA warning letter created 35% drawdown (temporary), Phase 3 readout as major catalyst, 8-10x 2028E EPS of $3.20 + $5-10/share pipeline value = 60-170% upside.

### Archetype 3: Corporate Change + Synergy
**Pattern:** Post-acquisition integration with quantifiable synergies that the market hasn't priced.
**Example:** Acquisition with synergy target 2x original estimate, already partially secured. Trades at 2.5x 2028E EBITDA vs. 8-10x target = 280-380% upside.

### Archetype 4: Structural Dislocation
**Pattern:** Identifiable structural quirk (regulatory, ownership, market structure) creating a temporary mispricing that will resolve on a known timeline.
**Example:** Subsidiary listed on foreign exchange, regulatory rules require 25% float within 3 years. Forced selling of stake at foreign multiples (40-50x P/E) vs. parent trading at 10x P/E creates value unlock.

### Archetype 5: SOTP Discount + Activism
**Pattern:** Conglomerate or holding company trading at deep discount to sum-of-parts, with activism as potential catalyst.

### Common Long-Side Thread
Every long position shares these characteristics:
1. **Per-share math is explicit** — not just "EBITDA will grow" but "FCF/share will be $X, at Y multiple = $Z target"
2. **Downside floor is identified** — asset value, cash, or stable base business
3. **Catalyst is specific and time-bound** — acquisition closing, Phase 3 readout, forced stake sale, synergy realization
4. **Capital allocation is value-accretive** — buybacks, disciplined M&A, debt paydown

## 7. Short-Side Forensic Methodology

The short book is run like forensic accounting married to liability analysis. Best targets are NOT just expensive stocks — they are businesses whose reported health depends on marks, non-cash income, fragile funding, or incentive structures that reward growth over truth.

### Forensic Signal Chain

| Signal | What to Test | Why It Matters |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Non-cash earnings | What share of income is PIK, mark-to-model, or non-cash? | Cash coverage weakens while reported coverage looks stable |
| Quarter-end optics | Do leverage/balance-sheet ratios improve only at reporting dates? | Possible temporary financing or cosmetic balance-sheet management |
| External management / fees | Does AUM growth or FRE dominate economic reality? | Pressure to keep dividends high and assets marked favorably |
| Funding mismatch | Can investors redeem faster than assets can be sold? | Confidence break becomes a flow problem |
| Aggressive add-backs | Are EBITDA adjustments recurring rather than truly one-time? | Leverage understated, covenants less comfortable than they seem |
| Opaque asset marks | How much value relies on stale comps, appraisals, or internal models? | NAV can move abruptly when forced sales occur |

### Six Reveal Events to Monitor

| Reveal Event | Why It Matters |
|-------------|----------------|
| Distribution/dividend cut | Management can no longer fund promised payout without taking more risk |
| Pro-rates, gates, redemption delays | Converts theoretical liquidity mismatch into visible funding problem |
| Failed or expensive refinancing | Raises cost of time, forces more realistic leverage math |
| Non-accrual spike / covenant amendment | Turns hidden stress into reported credit deterioration |
| Large asset sale below carrying value | Breaks confidence in internal marks |
| Board or management change | Can trigger fuller disclosure or strategic review |

### Short-Book Rules
- **Never** rely on "overvaluation" alone
- **Require** a path from hidden weakness to public recognition
- **Prefer** names where accounting, liability structure, AND incentives all point in the same adverse direction

## 8. Sector-Thesis Generation: Five-Part Template

This converts a broad narrative into a tradeable thesis:

| Workstream | Question | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| 1. Incentive Map | Who benefits from growth, leverage, FRE, or high marks? | Compensation decks, proxy filings, investor presentations |
| 2. Asset-Quality Map | Find weak credits, cyclical end markets, stale marks, aggressive assumptions | Underwriting comps, borrower disclosures, secondary prices |
| 3. Funding Map | Understand capital structure — what can run, reprice, or get gated? | Debt docs, vehicle terms, fund documents, maturity ladders |
| 4. Catalyst Map | List plausible reveal events and what metric each would move | Distribution coverage, non-accruals, spreads, secondary discounts |
| 5. Expression Choice | Choose cleanest instrument: single name, basket, pair, holdco, opco, or credit | Borrow cost, liquidity, downside convexity |

### Industry Vulnerability Checklist (10 Points)

Apply to any sector to assess systemic risk:

1. ☐ Incentive misalignment between managers and investors (fees based on AUM not performance)
2. ☐ Rapid AUM growth driven by fee economics rather than investment opportunity
3. ☐ Liquidity mismatch between assets and liabilities
4. ☐ Non-cash income (PIK, mark-to-model) supporting reported returns
5. ☐ Aggressive leverage with declining coverage ratios
6. ☐ Structured financing that can trap cash flows in stress (CLOs, repo-like facilities)
7. ☐ Quarter-end cosmetic balance sheet management
8. ☐ Retailization — selling illiquid assets to less sophisticated investors with liquidity expectations
9. ☐ Opacity — limited public information, inconsistent reporting standards
10. ☐ Historical pattern match to prior crises (SVB, 2008 structured credit)

## 9. Portfolio Construction & Risk Management

### Position Sizing

| Bucket | Use Case | Size Range |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Core long | High-quality business, clear discount, downside floor, catalyst visible | 4-8% gross |
| Catalyst long | More event-driven, timing-dependent, operationally messy but asymmetric | 2-5% gross |
| Thematic short basket | Sector/accounting thesis across multiple names | 0.75-2% each |
| Single-name forensic short | Strong reveal mechanism, borrow workable | 1-4% gross |
| Pair or hedge | Isolate company-specific alpha, reduce factor exposure | Variable |

### Sizing Formula

**Adjusted Size = Base Size × Liquidity Factor × Balance-Sheet Factor × Catalyst Factor × Correlation Factor**

- **Base size** from score bucket (2% at 75-79 pts, 4% at 80-89, 6% at 90+)
- **Liquidity factor** reduces for names that can't be exited in 3-5 trading days
- **Balance-sheet factor** reduces when survival depends on refinancing vs. current cash generation
- **Catalyst factor** reduces when thesis is correct but timing uncertain
- **Correlation factor** prevents accidental concentration in same macro/sector exposure

### Hard Limits

| Limit | Rule |
|-------|------|
| Single-name long | Rarely above 8% gross at cost unless downside floor is unusually hard |
| Single-name short | Rarely above 5% gross; tighter if borrow unstable or squeeze risk high |
| Sector net exposure | Cap where multiple positions share same catalyst path |
| Liquidity budget | 70-80% of book reducible inside one week under normal trading |
| Event concentration | Avoid too much P&L on one binary event (board vote, court ruling, financing window) |

### Drawdown Protocol
**Mandatory re-underwrite after 15-20% price move against thesis or any thesis-critical disclosure.**
- If thesis intact at better price → may increase position (re-entry at scale)
- If thesis impaired → reduce to zero immediately
- Exits are thesis-driven, not price-driven

## 10. Trade Management Patterns

Five distinct patterns:

### Pattern 1: Build-and-Trim
Large initial position, trim as price approaches target. Locks in gains while maintaining exposure.

### Pattern 2: Steady Build
Gradually increase position over multiple quarters as thesis confirms. Patience-driven.

### Pattern 3: Exit-and-Re-Entry (Drawdown Protocol)
Exit to zero when thesis is temporarily impaired, re-enter at larger size when thesis restored. **This is the most important pattern** — exits are thesis-driven, not price-driven.
- Re-entry can be 25-50% larger than original position — better price = better asymmetry = larger position

### Pattern 4: Tactical Trade
Short-duration position for specific catalyst, exit after event regardless of outcome.

### Pattern 5: Portfolio Hedge
Dynamic ETF hedging — can be large (25-30% of portfolio) or zero depending on market conditions. Not continuous.

### Review Cadence

| Cadence | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Daily | Price action vs. thesis, news flow, borrow changes, financing headlines, unusual volume |
| Weekly | Update catalyst log, check estimate revisions, review management statements, reassess peer moves |

| Quarterly | Full re-underwrite after results, management calls, material disclosures; decide hold/add/trim/exit |

### Dashboard Metrics by Book Segment

| Segment | Core Metrics |
|---------|-------------|
| Longs | FCF/share, leverage, buybacks, asset sales, margin progression, customer retention, management execution |
| Forensic shorts | Cash vs. reported income, non-accruals, distribution coverage, refinancing costs, asset marks, redemption/flow data |
| Thematic baskets | Common sector trigger metrics (spreads, defaults, volumes, pricing) |

### Exit Rules

1. **Exit when gap has materially closed** and remaining upside no longer compensates for residual risk
2. **Exit or reduce if catalyst fails** and thesis becomes timing-free value without a hard floor
3. **Exit immediately if key premise falsified:** balance sheet weaker than underwritten, management untrustworthy, or short reveal mechanism disappears

## 13. Mental Models

| Model | Application |
|-------|-------------|
| Reflexivity | Capital gathering rewarded more than capital appreciation creates self-reinforcing cycle until it breaks |
| Incentive-first analysis | "Follow the fees" — FRE at 30x vs. performance at <10x explains all behavior |
| SVB template | Illiquid overvalued assets + flighty funding = run risk. Apply to any vehicle with asset-liability mismatch |
| Retailization sequence | Institutional → wealth management → retail = late-cycle warning signal |
| PIK as stress indicator | Company with ANY tranche of PIK is far riskier than one paying all cash interest |
| Opacity as feature | "Why put financial information into the public domain if it might enable a trade that marks the asset below par?" |
| Uni-tranche illusion | First lien senior secured sounds safe but at 7x leverage on asset-light businesses, recoveries will be poor |
| "Too good to be true" dividend | Coverage ratio of exactly 1.0x including non-cash PIK = no real cash flow to support distribution |
| Confidence spiral | Pro-rate → massive redemption queues → severe liquidity issues → forced sales → marks collapse |
| Per-share vs. empire | Reject empire-building; show per-share accretion on every capital allocation decision |
| Structural dislocation | Regulatory/ownership quirks create temporary mispricings with known resolution timelines |
| Variant perception | "What does the market believe that you believe is wrong?" — required for every position |
